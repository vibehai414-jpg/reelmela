How a Hindi version gets made

Four steps. All free.

01

Auto-captions in English

CapCut: Import → Captions → Auto captions. Fix names and places by hand — auto-captions mangle “Harda” every time.

02

Translate to Hindi

CapCut's Translate turns captions into Hindi (Devanagari). Keep lines short — 2 lines max per screen, or nobody reads them at fest speed.

03

Add the Hindi voice

Best voice is yours: record the Hindi script over the clip in CapCut. Your own dub is also what makes the clip yours, not a re-upload.

04

Cut 3–5 clips, post across a week

Best, funny, crowd, money. One source reel becomes a week of Hindi posts — each one links back to the event page and the aftermovie.

CapCut — start here, ₹0

Free auto-captions + Hindi translate + your own voice dub in one app. No watermark on captions export. The robotic preset voice is the only weak spot — record your own.

YouTube auto-dubbing — ₹0

Upload the reel as Unlisted, YouTube generates a Hindi audio track viewers can switch to. Zero effort, but you don't control the voice or the wording.

HeyGen — lip-sync, with a catch

Best lip-sync for a talking-head clip, but the free tier caps out at about 1 minute per video — a 2-minute video does not fit free in one go. Split it, or use CapCut.

Clipping someone else's reel?
Ask first. In writing.

ReelMela event clips are yours to Hindi-dub — you shot them, or the event pool covers them. But a random creator's YouTube video or reel is not free to clip, famous or not. Credit is not permission. Copy a template, send it, keep their “yes”.

  • Only clip: your own footage, written permission, Creative Commons, or YouTube's own Remix tools
  • Your Hindi commentary must be the main part — a straight re-upload can be blocked and won't monetise
  • Always credit + link the original — as courtesy, not as protection
